Kerala Sadya Parippu Curry (Dal Curry)

Minija

A simple, creamy Kerala-style moong dal curry made with coconut and cumin, traditionally served with rice and a drizzle of ghee during a Kerala Sadya.

Easy • 25–30 mins • Main Dish • Veg

Ingredients

  • 1 cup split yellow moong dal (cherupayar parippu)
  • ¼ tsp turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 2–3 cups water, as needed

For the Coconut Paste

  • ½ cup grated coconut
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1–2 green chillies (optional)
  • A little water for grinding

For Tempering (Optional)

  • 1 tsp coconut oil
  • ½ tsp mustard seeds
  • 1–2 dried red chillies
  • 1 sprig curry leaves

To Serve

  • Ghee, as needed

Method

  1. Dry-roast the moong dal in a pan over low heat until lightly golden and aromatic. Stir continuously to prevent burning. Allow it to cool slightly.
  2. Wash the roasted dal and place it in a pressure cooker with turmeric powder and 2–3 cups of water.
  3. Pressure-cook until the dal is very soft and easily mashed. The cooking time depends on the cooker; usually, a few whistles are sufficient.
  4. Mash the cooked dal well. Add a little hot water if needed to achieve a thick, creamy consistency.
  5. Grind the grated coconut, cumin seeds and green chillies with a little water to make a smooth paste.
  6. Add the coconut paste to the mashed dal. Mix well and simmer gently for 3–5 minutes.
  7. Add salt to taste and adjust the consistency with hot water if necessary.
  8. If using tempering, heat coconut oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and let them splutter. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves, then pour over the dal.
  9. Serve hot with Kerala matta rice and a spoonful of ghee.

Notes

  • Roasting the dal gives it a lovely traditional flavour.
  • For an authentic Sadya-style taste, keep the seasoning simple and the dal thick but pourable.
  • Ghee added directly to the rice and dal is a classic way to serve it.
  • Some Sadya recipes omit the coconut paste and serve plain cooked moong dal with ghee. Both styles are enjoyed in Kerala.
