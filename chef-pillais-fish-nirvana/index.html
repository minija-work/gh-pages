Chef Pillai’s Fish Nirvana

Minija

 

A Kerala-style fish delicacy cooked in coconut milk with curry leaves, green chillies, ginger, and raw mango, traditionally served in a banana leaf.

Easy • 30 mins • Main Course • Non-Veg • Serve hot

Ingredients

For the fish

  • 300 g seer fish (kingfish) slices, or another large fish
  • 3 tbsp coconut oil, for shallow-frying

For the marinade

  • 2 tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 2 tsp coconut oil

For the coconut milk gravy

  • 1 banana leaf
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil, plus 1 tbsp for finishing
  • 2 sprigs curry leaves
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped ginger
  • 3 green chillies, sliced
  • ½ tsp black pepper powder
  • ½ raw mango, peeled and grated

Method

  1. Marinate the fish: Mix the red chilli powder, turmeric powder, salt, and 2 tsp coconut oil into a paste. Apply it evenly to the fish and leave to marinate for 15–20 minutes.
  2. Cook the fish: Heat 3 tbsp coconut oil in a pan and shallow-fry the fish for about 2 minutes on each side. Alternatively, air-fry until lightly cooked. Set aside.
  3. Prepare the banana leaf: Lightly pass the banana leaf over a flame to soften it and make it flexible. Line an earthen pot with the leaf.
  4. Add the aromatics: Pour 2 tbsp coconut oil into the pot and heat gently. Add the curry leaves.
  5. Add the fish: Place the fried fish in the pot on top of the curry leaves.
  6. Prepare the gravy: Add the coconut milk, chopped ginger, sliced green chillies, black pepper powder, and grated raw mango.
  7. Simmer gently: Cook on low heat for about 4 minutes, allowing the flavours to blend. Avoid boiling the coconut milk vigorously, as it may split.
  8. Finish: Drizzle the remaining 1 tbsp coconut oil over the top and turn off the heat.
  9. Serve hot with Kerala rice, appam, or bread.

Notes

  • You can use seer fish, another large fish cut into slices, or whole small fish.
  • Fresh coconut milk gives the dish a rich, authentic flavour.
  • Raw mango adds a pleasant tanginess. Adjust the quantity according to its sourness and your taste.
  • Handle the banana leaf carefully when heating it over a flame.
  • If using an earthen pot, heat it gradually to reduce the risk of cracking.
