Pumpkin and Cowpeas Curry (Pumpkin Erissery) – Chickoo’s Favourite

Minija

A traditional Kerala-style pumpkin erissery made with red cowpeas, coconut, and cumin, finished with golden-roasted coconut and a fragrant coconut oil tempering. A favourite dish served during the Onam sadya.

Medium • 50–60 mins • Main Course • Veg • Serve hot

Ingredients

For the curry

  • ½ kg pumpkin, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 125 g red cowpeas (vanpayar / unakkapayar), soaked overnight
  • ¼ tbsp turmeric powder
  • 1½ tbsp red chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp black pepper powder
  • Salt to taste
  • Water, as needed
  • 1 small piece jaggery (optional, if the pumpkin is not sweet)

For grinding

  • Grated coconut from ½ coconut (about 4 handfuls)
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds
  • A little water, as needed

For tempering

  • Grated coconut from ½ coconut (about 4 handfuls)
  • Mustard seeds, as needed
  • 2 dried red chillies, each cut into 2 pieces
  • ½ tbsp black pepper powder
  • Curry leaves, a few sprigs
  • Coconut oil, as needed
  • Ghee, a little

Method

1. Cook the cowpeas

  1. Drain the soaked cowpeas and transfer them to a pressure cooker.
  2. Add salt and enough water to cover the beans by about 2–3 cm.
  3. Cook on high heat until the first whistle, then reduce to medium heat and cook for another 5 minutes.
  4. Allow the pressure to release naturally. Check that the beans are tender.

2. Prepare the coconut paste

  1. Grind the grated coconut and cumin seeds with a little water into a fine paste.
  2. Set aside.

3. Cook the pumpkin

  1. Place the pumpkin pieces and cooked cowpeas in a pan.
  2. Add the turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper powder, salt, and a little water.
  3. Mix well, cover, and cook until the pumpkin becomes soft. Add water only as needed, as the pumpkin releases moisture while cooking.
  4. Lightly mash some of the pumpkin pieces and mix well to create a semi-dry gravy.

4. Add the coconut paste

  1. Add the ground coconut-cumin paste and mix thoroughly.
  2. Simmer over medium heat for a few minutes until the flavours blend and the curry reaches a thick, semi-dry consistency.
  3. Add a small piece of jaggery if the pumpkin lacks sweetness.
  4. Switch off the heat.

5. Prepare the roasted coconut tempering

  1. Heat coconut oil in a pan.
  2. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  3. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves. Fry briefly.
  4. Add the grated coconut and roast, stirring frequently, until golden brown.
  5. Push the roasted coconut mixture towards the sides of the pan and add a little ghee to the centre.
  6. Add the black pepper powder and mix well.
  7. Switch off the heat.

6. Finish the curry

  1. Pour the roasted coconut tempering over the pumpkin and cowpeas curry.
  2. Mix gently and serve hot with Kerala rice.

Notes

  • This dish is traditionally known as Pumpkin Erissery, a classic Kerala Onam sadya curry.
  • Coconut oil gives the curry its authentic Kerala flavour, while the roasted coconut adds a delicious nutty aroma.
  • Adjust the chilli and pepper powders to your preferred spice level.
  • The curry should be thick and semi-dry rather than watery.
  • If using dried cowpeas, soaking them overnight helps them cook evenly.
  • Jaggery is optional; use it only if the pumpkin is not naturally sweet.
