Egg Biryani 🥚

Minija

A flavourful Kerala-style biryani made with boiled eggs, aromatic spices, fresh herbs, and fragrant rice. The eggs are lightly fried before being coated in the masala for extra flavour.

Medium • 60–75 minutes • Vegetarian-friendly alternative available • Serves 4

Ingredients

For the rice

  • Basmati rice – 2 cups (or jeerakasala rice for Kerala style)
  • Water – as needed
  • Salt – to taste
  • Bay leaves – 2
  • Star anise – 1, optional

For the eggs

  • Eggs – 6, boiled and peeled
  • A pinch of turmeric powder
  • A pinch of red chilli powder
  • Salt – to taste
  • Oil – for frying

For the egg masala

  • Onion – 3 large, thinly sliced
  • Tomato – 2 medium, chopped
  • Green chillies – 4
  • Ginger – 1-inch piece
  • Garlic – 8–10 cloves
  • Turmeric powder – ½ tsp
  • Red chilli powder – 1 tsp
  • Coriander powder – 1 tbsp
  • Black pepper powder – ½ tsp
  • Garam masala – 1 tsp
  • Curd (yoghurt) – 2 tbsp
  • Mint leaves – 1 cup
  • Coriander leaves – 1 cup
  • Ghee – 3 tbsp
  • Oil – as needed
  • Salt – to taste
  • Whole spices – cloves (3), cardamom (3), cinnamon (1-inch piece), fennel seeds (½ tsp), star anise (1, optional), and mace (1 small piece, optional)

For the garnish

  • Fried onions
  • Fried cashews
  • Fried raisins
  • Extra mint and coriander leaves
  • Lemon juice – 1 tbsp
  • A pinch of turmeric mixed with 1 tbsp water, for colour

Method

1. Prepare the eggs

  1. Make small slits in the boiled eggs.
  2. Heat a little oil in a pan.
  3. Add the eggs with a pinch of turmeric powder, chilli powder, and salt.
  4. Fry gently until lightly golden. Set aside.

2. Cook the rice

  1. Bring water to a boil with salt, the bay leaves, and star anise, if using.
  2. Add the washed rice and cook until approximately 70% done.
  3. Drain the rice and remove the whole spices. Set aside.

3. Prepare the masala

  1. Grind the ginger, garlic, green chillies, whole spices (no bay leaf and star anise)  into a paste.
  2. Heat oil and some ghee in a heavy-bottomed pot.
  3. Fry one sliced onion until golden brown. Remove and reserve for the garnish.
  4. Fry the cashews until golden, then fry the raisins until they puff up. Set aside with the fried onions.
  5. Add the remaining onions and sauté until soft and translucent.
  6. Add the ground paste and sauté until fragrant.
  7. Add the tomatoes and cook until soft.
  8. Add the turmeric, chilli powder, coriander powder, pepper powder, garam masala, and salt. Mix well.
  9. Add the curd and cook until the masala thickens and the oil begins to separate.
  10. Add the mint and coriander leaves.

4. Add the eggs

  1. Add the fried eggs to the masala.
  2. Gently turn them so they are coated with the spices.
  3. Cook for 2–3 minutes.

5. Layer the biryani

  1. Spread the egg masala evenly in the pot.
  2. Layer the partially cooked rice over the masala.
  3. Sprinkle the fried onions, cashews, raisins, mint, and coriander leaves over the rice.
  4. Drizzle the remaining ghee and lemon juice over the top.
  5. Sprinkle the turmeric-water mixture over the rice for colour.

6. Dum cooking

  1. Cover the pot tightly.
  2. Place a tawa underneath and cook over very low heat for 15–20 minutes.
  3. Turn off the heat and let the biryani rest for 5–10 minutes before serving.

Notes

  • Lightly frying the boiled eggs gives them more flavour and helps the spices coat the surface.
  • For a different version, scramble the eggs with salt and spices and mix them into the masala instead of using boiled eggs.
  • Use plenty of mint and fried onions for a traditional Kerala-style flavour.
  • Ghee adds richness and aroma to the biryani.
  • Avoid overcooking the rice during the first stage, as it will continue cooking during dum.
