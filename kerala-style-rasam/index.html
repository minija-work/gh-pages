Kerala-Style Rasam

Minija

 

A tangy, peppery South Indian rasam made with tamarind, tomatoes, garlic, and freshly ground spices. A comforting dish to enjoy with rice or as a warm soup.

Easy • 25–30 mins • Side Dish • Veg • Serve hot

Ingredients

  • 3 small tomatoes or 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 25 g tamarind (about 1 lemon-sized piece)
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 3 green chillies, slit lengthwise
  • 10 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp coriander seeds
  • 20 curry leaves, divided
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 tsp asafoetida powder
  • 2 dried red chillies, halved
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • ½ tsp red chilli powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil, preferably coconut oil
  • ¼ cup chopped coriander leaves
  • 4 cups water, divided

Method

1. Prepare the tamarind and tomatoes

  1. Soak the tamarind in 1 cup of warm water for about 10 minutes.
  2. Squeeze out the juice, strain it, and discard the pulp.
  3. Chop the tomatoes and mash them into the tamarind juice.

2. Prepare the spice mixture

  1. Grind the garlic, cumin seeds, black peppercorns, and coriander seeds into a coarse paste. Add a little water if needed.

3. Cook the rasam

  1. Transfer the tamarind-tomato mixture to a saucepan.
  2. Add the slit green chillies, turmeric powder, red chilli powder, asafoetida powder, salt, ground spice mixture, and the remaining 3 cups of water.
  3. Add about 10 curry leaves.
  4. Bring to a gentle simmer over low to medium heat, stirring occasionally.
  5. When the rasam begins to foam and rise, switch off the heat. Avoid prolonged boiling, as this can reduce its fresh, aromatic flavour.

4. Prepare the tempering

  1. Heat the oil in a small pan.
  2. Add the mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  3. Add the halved dried red chillies and fry briefly.
  4. Switch off the heat and add the remaining curry leaves.
  5. Pour the tempering over the cooked rasam.

5. Finish and serve

  1. Taste and adjust the salt if necessary.
  2. Garnish with chopped coriander leaves.
  3. Serve hot with steamed rice or enjoy as a soup.

Notes

  • Adjust the black pepper and green chillies to suit your preferred spice level.
  • Coconut oil adds a traditional Kerala flavour.
  • Switch off the heat as soon as the rasam foams; vigorous boiling can make the flavour less fresh.
  • For a stronger garlic flavour, lightly crush the garlic before grinding.
  • Rasam tastes especially good when served fre   sh with rice, a vegetable side dish, and pappadam
