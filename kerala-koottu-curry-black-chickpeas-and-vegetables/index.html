Kerala Koottu Curry (Black Chickpeas and Vegetables)

Minija

 

A traditional Kerala sadya dish made with black chickpeas, raw banana, yam, and coconut, finished with roasted coconut for a nutty flavour.

Medium • 50–60 mins • Side Dish • Veg • Serve hot

Ingredients

  • ½ cup black chickpeas (kadala), soaked overnight
  • 1 medium raw banana, peeled and cubed
  • 1 cup elephant yam (chena), cubed
  • 2 green chillies, slit
  • ¼ tsp turmeric powder
  • ½ tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp black pepper powder
  • Salt to taste
  • Water, as needed

For grinding

  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 3–4 dried red chillies

For roasting

  • 3 tbsp grated coconut

For tempering

  • 2 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 2 dried red chillies
  • 1 sprig curry leaves

Method

  1. Drain the soaked black chickpeas. Pressure-cook with enough water until tender. Allow the pressure to release naturally.
  2. Place the raw banana and yam in a pan with turmeric powder, red chilli powder, black pepper powder, salt, and enough water to cook.
  3. Cover and cook until the vegetables are tender.
  4. Grind the grated coconut, cumin seeds, and dried red chillies into a coarse paste, using as little water as possible.
  5. Add the cooked chickpeas and ground coconut paste to the vegetables. Mix well.
  6. Simmer for 5–7 minutes until the flavours blend and the gravy becomes semi-thick.
  7. In a separate pan, dry-roast 3 tbsp grated coconut until golden brown.
  8. Add the roasted coconut to the curry and mix well.
  9. Heat coconut oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  10. Add the dried red chillies and curry leaves. Fry briefly and pour the tempering over the curry.
  11. Mix gently and serve hot.

Notes

  • Adjust the thickness with a little hot water if needed. The curry should be semi-thick.
  • Roasted coconut gives koottu curry its characteristic nutty flavour.
  • Make sure the black chickpeas are fully tender before combining them with the vegetables.
  • Serve with rice, olan, avial, and pappadam as part of a Kerala sadya.
